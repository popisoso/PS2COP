The Challenge

How can your organization find a location that is not only suitable for your activities and target audience, but also affordable? This is one of the biggest challenges many organizations face. Yet, several members of the Community of Practice have managed to find suitable and affordable locations in various ways.

How do you make sure you find a fitting location?

From Experience: What (Not) To Do

  • Few organizations manage to have their own building. It often costs a lot of money.
  • Avoid tunnel vision and don’t focus on finding one single location for all your activities. Be willing to make compromises. For example, be open to offering different activities at different locations.
  • Find another organization or foundation with whom you can rent and share a space. Make clear agreements about who will use the space and when.
  • Keep in mind that locations have their own rules about what you can and cannot do there. Sometimes you find a location, but it turns out not to be suitable for your activity or gathering.
  • If you find a location you share with other organizations, take other users into account.

Step-By-Step Plan

  1. Step 1: Create a search profile

    First, clarify exactly what you are looking for. The help questions can assist you with this. To find a space more quickly, it may be useful to offer different activities at different locations.

  2. Step 2: Reach out

    Contact the Community Builder, neighborhood manager, or WJP representative in the neighborhood where you are organizing the activity. They can connect you with local contacts and provide information about available spaces and subsidies.

  3. Step 3: Look into community rooms

    Find out if there are any community rooms in the neighborhood or district. Ask if you can use them. Community rooms are often not suitable for large activities or gatherings, but they are suitable for meetings.

  4. Step 4: Join meetings

    Join neighborhood meetings to stay informed, discuss your needs, and connect with neighborhood organizations.

  5. Step 5: Create an overview

    Make an overview of neighborhood organizations and community centers with their own buildings. Search online or visit them, introduce yourself, and inquire about rentals and prices.

  6. Step 6: Location found

    Once you have found a suitable location, make sure you know immediately who the manager is who also has the keys/access. This will save time when your activity or meeting is about to start.

Checklist

Do’s And Don’t’s

DO

  • Create a cost overview of your ideal location in advance.
    Think about rent, insurance, gas/water/electricity, and renovation. The clearer and more concrete you are, the better the manager/landlord/subsidy provider will understand you.

  • Make your search profile as specific as possible: What are your requirements? How big should the location be? How much can you pay? And what is its intended purpose?
    (Tip: if it has a cultural purpose, you may qualify for specific subsidies such as the cultural incubator subsidy, and you can even apply for funding for the first months of rent and renovation.)

DON’T

  • Renting locations that are far beyond your budget or for which you would have to take out a loan.
