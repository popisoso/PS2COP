The Challenge

A group of women in the neighborhood come together and notice that there are few activities for women. They start working with limited resources and become successful. Now the question is: how do we move forward as an organization?

What does setting up an organization involve?

From Experience: What (Not) To Do

At PEP, a lot of information is available about how to establish a foundation, but in practice, the implementation can be challenging.
Members of the CoP have identified various advantages and disadvantages of starting a foundation:

Disadvantages:

  • Establishing a foundation takes time and commitment. This can be demanding if you’re doing it voluntarily alongside, for example, your job.
  • A foundation comes with responsibilities. Not everyone wants to carry that responsibility.
  • A foundation can also be vulnerable because it relies on volunteers. Finding replacements for departing board members can be a major challenge.

Advantages:

  • Other organizations are more likely to find and connect with you if you are a foundation.
  • In practice, as a group, you are taken more seriously and recognized when you are a foundation.
  • Access to networks becomes easier when you represent a foundation.
  • As a foundation, you have more opportunities to apply for funding and subsidies.
  • You can recruit volunteers more effectively if your foundation has a clear purpose.

Building a strong community in your neighborhood, district, or environment is advantageous before setting up a foundation. That means more people will be willing to support you when you start one.

Step-By-Step Plan

  1. Step 1: Volunteer involvement

    Involve volunteers in the change process and prepare them for the new organizational structure.

  2. Step 2: Establish a core group

    Establish a core group for the establishment of the foundation.

  3. Step 3: Analyze the situation

    Analyze the situation: how does it work now and what is going well?

  4. Step 4: The pros and cons

    Investigate the pros and cons of establishing a foundation.

  5. Step 5: Paperwork

    Take care of the paperwork for the foundation and decide who does what.

  6. Step 6: Mission & vision

    Work together to develop a clear vision and mission for the foundation. What do you stand for? What is your goal? What do you want to achieve and why do you want to achieve it? How are you going to achieve your goal?

  7. Step 7: Board members

    Discuss what type of organizational board you need and which board members are suitable for it.

  8. Step 8: Costs

    Consider how you will cover the start-up costs, such as notary fees.

  9. Step 9: Address registration

    Find an address for registration with the Chamber of Commerce.

  10. Step 10: Process supervisor

    Consider a process supervisor if it is difficult to reach agreement.

Checklist

Do’s And Don’t’s

DO

  • Start a conversation with other foundations that inspire you. How did they approach this process?

  • Also research other legal forms, such as a cooperative or an association. Sometimes you can see if your organisation can fall under an existing foundation that shares the same mission. Be sure to make clear agreements in that case.

DON’T

  • Don’t assume that everyone can or wants to become a board member.

  • Don’t become a foundation just because you think it will make it easier to get subsidies.
